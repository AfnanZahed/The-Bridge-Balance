# JOB 5 — How the strongest AI tutors are built, and why

Legend: **V** = exact quote verified on the fetched official page; **U** = claim I could not verify in checked sources; **NOT FOUND** = searched and absent (sources checked named). Quotes are fragments (≤20 words).

## 1. CS50's AI tutor (CS50 Duck / cs50.ai / `ddb`)

**What they do → Why**
- Duck is "an adaptation of ChatGPT" at cs50.ai, also embedded in VS Code — "supports rubber duck debugging and is thus a 'duck debugger,' or `ddb`". It is "designed to behave more like a good tutor, leading students toward answers rather than spoiling them outright" ([docs](https://cs50.readthedocs.io/cs50.ai/)) — V.
- Stated why: approximate "a 1:1 teacher-to-student ratio"; the paper's goal was "guiding students, not disclosing answers directly, and staying up-to-date with course changes" ([SIGCSE 2024](https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/V1fp0567-liu.pdf), §2) — V. Students described it as "a personal tutor" (abstract) — V.
- Prompt design: a "custom system prompt that tells the model to act as a teaching assistant for CS50", plus "multiple configuration files (in YAML format) with different system prompts and user prompt templates" per use case (§4.1) — V. Guardrails are "built-in 'pedagogical guardrails'" aligned to "guiding students, rather than providing direct solutions" (§3) — V.
- Usage limiting ("hearts"): "each student starts with 10 hearts and regains one heart every three minutes"; each interaction consumes one (§4.6) — V. Stated reasoning: cost control **and** pedagogy — it "promotes thoughtful interaction… encouraging students to carefully consider their questions" and "encourages reflective breaks"; they refused to remove throttling despite student requests — V.
- Academic honesty: policy says it is "unreasonable (i.e., academically dishonest) to use AI-based software other than CS50's own (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, et al.)… except when explicitly allowed by a course" — while "it is reasonable to use CS50's own AI-based software" ([docs](https://cs50.readthedocs.io/cs50.ai/)) — V.
- Prompt-injection handling: a "guard" checks "every student request for atypical non-alphanumeric patterns"; if triggered, an independent GPT-4 call decides, and if confirmed "the system immediately aborts the current user session" (2024, §4.4) — V. Also "any personally identifiable information (PII) is removed" before prompting (§4) — V.
- Grounding: RAG over 30-second lecture-caption segments in ChromaDB to "reduce the chances of 'hallucination'" (§4.3) — V.
- Published evaluation: senior staff reviewed 64 Duck answers — "22 out of 25 (88%) curricular answers were correct", "30 out of 39 (77%) administrative answers were correct" (2024, §5.2) — V. The 2025 follow-up found "instruction dilution" at scale: system prompts "close to 1,000 tokens"; "approximately 2.1 million responses—22% of all interactions—contained code blocks"; at conversation level 48% ([SIGCSE 2025](https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/fp0627-liu.pdf)) — V.
- Evaluation method (2025): TF pairwise "battle" on 50 queries + an LLM judge using OpenAI Evals with the question "Which response is from the better tutor?"; the judge matched human preference "82% of the time" — V. Improvements: few-shot (V2) and fine-tuning a GPT-4o-mini "trained on 50 example conversations over 5 epochs" (V3); multi-turn testing showed TFs "preferred conversations generated by the models with few-shot prompting and fine-tuning over the original version (V0) 60% of the time" — V. Cost benchmark: "10 million queries at an average cost of $1.50 per student per year" — V.

## 2. Khanmigo (Khan Academy)

- **What → Why:** "Unlike ChatGPT, Khanmigo never gives you the answer. It's built to help you learn"; "with limitless patience, it guides learners to find the answer themselves" ([learners page](https://www.khanmigo.ai/learners)) — V.
- Prompt structure, per their [7-step write-up](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academys-7-step-approach-to-prompt-engineering-for-khanmigo/): (1) define the ideal tutor relationship, (2) consult learning-science literature to keep students at their "'Goldilocks' learning edge", (3) inject account/course/interests from Khan's library, (4) engineer tone, personality, question type, (5) test with "diverse user personas" (personas surfaced an emoji-in-dark-topics failure; prompts were rewritten), (6) safety, (7) iterate on user feedback (repetition complaints → "economy of language") — all V.
- Moderation: "moderation guardrails that will stop inappropriate conversations and immediately inform parents and teachers" (blog) — V. Also automatic email: "sends an automatic email alert to an adult" ([AI guidelines](https://blog.khanacademy.org/aiguidelines/)) — V.
- Accuracy safeguards: stated mitigations are "Fine-tuning the AI to help increase accuracy" and "Prompt engineering to guide and narrow the focus of the AI", plus moderation monitoring and "'Red teaming' … to uncover potential vulnerabilities" (guidelines) — V. A dedicated math-verification engine is **NOT FOUND** (checked guidelines, 7-step blog, learners page). AI limitations are disclosed: "AI can be wrong and may generate inappropriate content. AI can make errors in math" (guidelines) — V.
- Access/safety gates: Khan Labs users "must be at least 18 years old"; children only via parental consent; chat history visible to parents/teachers (guidelines) — V. Learner signup: "be 18 years or older"; otherwise "your parent or guardian needs to sign up"; one parent may enable "up to 10 of your children" (learners page) — V.
- Usage limits: "We limit the amount of interaction individuals can have with the AI per day because we have observed that extended interactions are more likely to lead to poor AI behavior" (guidelines) — V.
- Evaluation: persona testing + prompt rewrites driven by user reports (blog) — V; a public tutor-quality rubric is **NOT FOUND** in the checked Khan sources.

## 4. Duolingo Max (Explain My Answer, Roleplay)

- **Explain My Answer** (started in Max, "now free for all Duolingo learners") gives a button after any answer, "personalized feedback that takes into account the error you made", learner choice: "Choose whether you want feedback on a mistake" ([blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/explain-my-answer-now-free/)) — V.
- **Roleplay** is bounded by the curriculum: "Humans write the scenarios that learners see in Roleplay"; experts "write the initial message in the chat and tell the model where to take the conversation"; scenarios "aligned with where the learner is in their course" ([Max blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max/)) — V.
- Quality control and honesty: "We also constantly review AI-generated content to ensure that the answers are factually correct"; "technology is never perfect"; users report errors "by holding down the inaccurate message" and "Our team will watch for these reports and use them to train the model to be more accurate" — V.

## 6. Safety for teenage/young-adult learners

- OpenAI: under-18s are "automatically placed into ChatGPT for Teens"; age-appropriate protections in "self-harm, violence, eating disorders… explicit sexual or graphic content"; "responsible homework reminders, which can recognize when a teen appears to be trying to shortcut an assignment and redirect them toward Study Mode"; Study Hours managed by parents; "break reminders" — V ([Teens](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/)).
- Prompt injection: OpenAI's stated doctrine is to "design the system so that the impact of manipulation is constrained, even if some attacks succeed", confirming/blocking suspicious transmissions (Safe Url) rather than relying only on input filters — V ([blog](https://openai.com/index/designing-agents-to-resist-prompt-injection/)). CS50 instead pattern-guards inputs and aborts the session — V (SIGCSE 2024 §4.4).
- Khan: moderation with adult alerts, red teaming, parental consent, 18+ self-signup — V (guidelines).
- Anthropic for under-18s: orgs must implement "Age verification systems", moderation, "Monitoring and reporting mechanisms", and "we may provide a child-safety system prompt"; "Anthropic will periodically audit organizations" — V ([minors guidelines](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344)).
- Refusing graded work: ChatGPT help says "For graded work, follow the AI-use policies of your school, instructor, or organization" — V; Teens page redirects shortcutting — V. Explicit graded-work refusal by Khanmigo/Duolingo: **NOT FOUND**.

## 7. How these teams evaluate tutor quality

- CS50 (most detailed public practice): a 50-query golden set ("15 queries on code generation, 15 on debugging, 10 on error messages, 5 on introductory coding concepts, and 5 on conceptual CS questions"); human pairwise single-turn and multi-turn arena evaluations by TFs (1,309 comparisons from 29 TFs; later 480 comparisons from 72 TFs); an LLM judge ("Which response is from the better tutor?", via OpenAI Evals) validated at "82%" agreement with humans; Elo ratings via ChatBot Arena code; and multi-turn preferred over single-turn because it "more accurately reflects real-world student-AI interactions" — V. Conclusion: "human evaluators—especially those who were recently CS50 students themselves—are best suited to this task" — V. Model upgrades are treated as regressions risk (GPT-4→4o raised code generation) — V.
- OpenAI: a randomized study with "over 300 college students" (control vs two study-mode variants; intention-to-treat): microeconomics "roughly a 15% higher score relative" to control, neuroscience "not distinguishable"; plus the Learning Outcomes Measurement Suite (learning-interaction classifiers, learning-quality graders, longitudinal graders, standardized cognitive measures); validation with ~20,000 Estonian students aged 16–18 — V ([blog](https://openai.com/index/understanding-ai-and-learning-outcomes/)).
- Khan: personas + monitoring/evaluation plan + iterative prompt rewrites from reports — V; Duolingo: expert-written scenarios, constant factual review, learner reports feeding model improvement — V. Anthropic: no public tutor-quality evaluation methodology found — **NOT FOUND**.

## What all of them missed or do badly

- **Guardrails leak in practice.** CS50 measured it: 22% of responses (48% of conversations) contained code blocks despite explicit "no solutions" instructions — self-published proof that prompt-only guardrails fail at scale.
- **Dose limits are resented, not taught.** CS50 students asked for throttling to be reduced; none of the vendors frames limits as a metacognitive exercise the way CS50 tries to.
- **Evaluation is mostly in-house and non-comparable.** Every team self-grades (CS50's Elo rig, OpenAI's suitemetrics, Khan's personas); no cross-vendor rubric exists.
- **Mode discoverability is poor.** ChatGPT needs `@study` or a buried `+` menu; Gemini hides Guided Learning under More tools; Claude's Learning mode lives inside Projects — hard for absolute beginners.
- **No public accuracy baseline.** Only CS50 publishes answer-correctness numbers (88%/77%); businesses publish none.
- **False confidence is acknowledged but unfixed.** CS50: AI "exhibits a tone of complete and authoritative confidence even when wrong"; Khan: "AI can make errors in math" — both ship anyway.
- **Younger-learner safety is gating, not pedagogy.** Khan required 18+/parent consent; Anthropic's answer is an org checklist; OpenAI's teen layer arrived years after teen use began.
- **Accessibility and basic chat controls are undocumented.** Copy-code, stop/regenerate, streaming, and accessibility behaviour are absent from official tutor docs (I checked OpenAI help, Google help/blog, Anthropic announcement/help, Duolingo blog).

## Recommendations for our MVP 1.0

1. **Don't rely on one long system prompt.** CS50 showed instruction dilution at ~1,000 tokens; keep ours short, add 3–5 few-shot exemplars, and plan prompt-routing/fine-tuning later (finding: dilution).
2. **Instrument solution leakage from day one.** Log and count code blocks / full answers per response (CS50's ` ``` c ` detector); set an internal ceiling below CS50's 22%.
3. **Make the mode picker the front door**, not hidden: visible "Guide me / Explain / Quiz me" for beginners (finding: discoverability). Free text stays available.
4. **Copy CS50's hearts idea but gentler**: e.g., 15 regenerating "questions", with a short line explaining why (thinking time + cost) (finding: dose limits; CS50: 10 hearts/3 min).
5. **Persistent, honest limitations line** ("I can be wrong — verify against the book") plus a starting-risk notice before the first message (Khan's "read and accept"; Duolingo's "never perfect").
6. **Input guard + canary for prompt injection**, and treat anything pasted from the internet as untrusted; on attack, deflect and log rather than hard-abort sessions (CS50's guard; OpenAI's "constrain impact" doctrine).
7. **Refuse graded assignments by default**; when a student asks for "the answer", redirect to a question + hint, like OpenAI's homework reminders (finding: graded work).
8. **Ship thumb up/down + free-text + a specific "wrong/unsafe" report path**, and actually review reports weekly (Duolingo's hold-to-report → retraining).
9. **Build a 50-question golden set now** (beginner C/Python/JS bugs, error messages, concept questions), and have a TA-grade it pairwise — season 1 of our own "better tutor?" rig (CS50's exact recipe).
10. **Adopt a model-as-judge for prompt A/B only after it agrees with human graders ~80%** on that golden set; prefer multi-turn arena comparisons over single-turn (CS50's 82% benchmark).
11. **For our teenage readers**: age-gate quietly, enable moderation with escalation, add break reminders, and never let the agent imply human feelings (Anthropic minors checklist; OpenAI teen protections).
12. **Budget to CS50's yardstick**: ≈US$0.05/query, ≈US$1.50/student/year, and note CS50 ran unlimited-use models successfully at that cost — plan our free tier around per-question caps (finding: cost).
