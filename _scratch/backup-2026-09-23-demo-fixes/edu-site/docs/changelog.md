# Changelog

Newest first, and every change carries its date. Only changes you would notice are here: a chapter going live, a stage being renamed, a page being added, a fix you can see. Small work behind the scenes is left out. The book is written and maintained by one person, [Afnan Zahed](/welcome#whos-behind-this), and this page is updated whenever something ships.

<Callout type="info" title="How to read this page">
Every change sits under one of four words. **New** is something that did not exist before. **Changed** is something that works differently now. **Fixed** is something that was wrong and is right now. **Removed** is something that is gone.
</Callout>

### 2026-09-22

#### Changed

- **The course now has three stages.** Stage 0 is *Introduction to SDE*, Stage 1 is *SDE Mastery (AI-Driven)*, and Stage 2 is *SDE Mastery (AI-Native)*. Agentic AI, meaning AI software that works on its own to get something done, is no longer a stage of its own: it is taught inside software engineering, so directing AI coding agents is learned alongside the engineering rather than in a later part of the course. Every stage list on the site, the [Welcome](/welcome) page, the menu, the footer, the homepage cards and the [FAQ](/faq#what-are-the-stages), now says the same three things in the same order.
- **The CS50 pages are off the site while Credentials is redesigned.** Credentials is no longer a stage. It is a separate track that runs alongside the three stages, and it is switched off for now. The two Harvard certificates, CS50P and CS50W, are unchanged: both courses are free to complete, and the verified certificate that makes one printable and checkable is optional and paid. What they are and what they cost is still answered in the [FAQ](/faq#what-does-the-cs50-certification-cost).
- **This changelog page has a new layout.** Every entry is grouped under New, Changed, Fixed or Removed, and every claim was checked against the record.

### 2026-09-21

#### Changed

- **The [Welcome](/welcome) page names the stages, and it carries the safety floor.** The stage names on the page matched the book but not the menu or the footer, "Foundations" in one place and "Spec-Aware Vibe Engineering Foundations" in another, and all of them agreed after this change. The plain-words safety-floor box now sits on the Welcome page too: whatever the AI writes for you is not finished until you have checked it yourself. One note under "How to read this book" was rewritten in plain words, and the closing section is now titled "Start reading" instead of "Ready?".

### 2026-09-20

#### Changed

- **The four "why this book" cards on the homepage are cards now.** They had no styling at all, so the four headings and the four paragraphs under them ran together as one long column of text with nothing to separate one pillar from the next. Each is now a raised panel with a lit top edge, its number set large and faint in the corner, and its icon in a small raised chip of its own. The four top edges step from light grey to black in order, the same descent down the site's grey ramp that the stages use, so the row reads as one set. Moving the pointer over a panel brings a soft light with it.
- **Both new Stage 0 chapters have been rewritten.** [How a Machine Holds a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [How People Told a Machine What to Do](/ch02-programming-is-born) now open by welcoming you, saying what the chapter covers and why it matters, and then move into the teaching gradually instead of dropping you into it. Each one is carried by one everyday thing you can picture the whole way through: a torch signalled across a dark street in the first, and a tireless helper with no judgment at all in the second. Hard words the ideas did not need are gone, so a transistor is "a tiny piece of specially treated crystal" now, not "a sliver of treated germanium". The history, the loom, the three kinds of switch, the rewiring of ENIAC and the arrival of FORTRAN are all still there.
- **Chapter and section titles now say what they are about.** "Yes, No, and Nothing In Between" is now "Why a Computer Only Says Yes or No". In the menu, "1. Foundations" is now "1. How a Machine Holds a Number" and "2. Programming Is Born" is now "2. How People Told a Machine What to Do". A title should tell you what is inside before you open it.
- **Stage 0 was given a clearer name.** It had been "Orientation Through Computing History". It became **Introduction to Computing: From Switches to AI Agents**, the same stage, named so you could tell what it covered without reading it first.
- **The bold-every-first-sentence style is gone.** From 16 September, the opening sentence of almost every paragraph in the book was set in bold. The intention was to make a page easier to scan. The effect was that nothing stood out, because everything did. Bold now marks what actually matters, wherever in a paragraph it falls, and pages are made easier to scan with bullets, tables and shorter sections instead.
- **The glossary stops talking to itself.** Entries used to carry notes written for the book's own authors, "Beginner · Summary" and "Advanced · Detailed" among them. Those are gone; each entry now simply says where in the book you first meet the term.

### 2026-09-16

#### New

- **The first two chapters of a rebuilt Stage 0 have shipped.** They are [How a Machine Holds a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [How People Told a Machine What to Do](/ch02-programming-is-born). They open a new, single line of chapters, in the order computing developed, with nothing taught before the thing it depends on, running from a two-state switch through the first working compiler in 1957. The book's original five chapters stay live while the rest of the new line is written. These two chapters were not linked from the menu until the next day.

#### Changed

- **A chapter's prose is set to a readable line length.** The reading column used to spread to whatever width the screen allowed, which at a desktop size put about 98 characters on a line, well past the 45 to 75 that reading comfortably holds, and that was the main reason a dense page read as a wall. It was capped so a line carried about 70 characters at a wide size, and the column sat centred with an even margin either side instead of pressed against the left of the leftover space. Nothing moved on a phone or a small laptop, where the line was already inside the band.
- **Chapter titles and section headings are heavier, larger and underlined.** Every heading level in use steps up 10%, reads at bold weight, and carries a hairline rule under it that runs the width of the text block, so a title that wraps to three lines gets one rule under the block, not one rule per line. The title's old short accent bar is gone, because two rules under one title is one rule too many.
- **About one sentence in five is set in bold, on one rule.** The rule is the opening sentence of a paragraph, which is the sentence that says what the paragraph is about. Emphasis the text already carried is left exactly where it was, and a paragraph whose first sentence is a link or already emphasised takes the next sentence instead. This style was retired on 20 September.
- **A glossary term is marked quietly now instead of loudly.** It used to carry four signals at once, the display serif, an accent colour, a full-strength underline and a tinted background, which made a defined word read as a foreign object inside its own sentence. It stays in the reading ink and is marked by one thing: a fine dotted accent underline that turns to the accent colour when you hover it. Ordinary links are unchanged, so a term and a link no longer look alike at a glance.
- **Italics are emphasis again, not a change of typeface.** The display serif is kept for the one surface that wants it: a pull-quote inside a chapter, which now sets larger in that serif with a short accent rule above it, and no left border. List bullets take the same small accent diamond the headings use, and the space between paragraphs, headings, tables, quotes and code now steps by a single scale instead of eight unrelated numbers.
- **The reading column's side margins are about 30% smaller.** The column used to be a fixed width, which had two consequences: the empty margin was 119px per side at a desktop size, and folding the left sidebar made that margin wider, 216px, while the text stayed exactly where it was. The column now holds a constant margin and lets the text fill everything else, so it grows into whatever the panels free up and stops there. The line lands around 80 characters at a desktop size, against 71 before; a narrower margin would have reached 85, which is why it stops short of a 50% cut.
- **The On this page panel folds away now.** A handle at its head names the action, and folded it leaves the same 44px rail carrying the same mark aimed the other way, so either edge of the page can be cleared for full-width reading. It moves on the same 200ms clock as the sidebar rail, holds its own contrast in both light and dark mode, and remembers being folded as you move between chapters. The text widens to meet the space and narrows again when the panel returns.

### 2026-09-15

#### New

- **A [Welcome](/welcome) page opens the book, ahead of Stage 0.** It states the founding claim, what "balance" actually means and why checking an AI agent's work cannot move to the machine, then what you need to start (nothing), how the stages fit together, how a chapter is meant to be read, an honest list of what the site does not have yet (no community, no help chatbot, no certificate), and who actually writes and maintains the book.

#### Changed

- **The Welcome page's key terms link to the [Glossary](/glossary) at their first use.** *Coding agent* and *Spec-Driven Engineering* are the two. Its "why can't the checking move to the machine" section now names the gap between what the word "agent" implies, something that decides on its own, and what actually runs, software whose independence is a setting you control rather than a trait it has.
- **The reading page's type was rebalanced so headings lead it again.** Body prose, code, tables, callouts, the menu and the On this page list all stepped down together, and the line length stepped down with them so a line still held the same number of characters.
- **The sidebar's collapse control is a labelled handle now, and the space it leaves behind is a designed rail.** Collapsing used to leave a blank 30px strip with a grey arrow floating at its centre, while the control that did the collapsing was a full-width bar with square corners and a chevron pointing at nothing. Closing the panel is now a capsule at the panel's foot that names what it does, and the collapsed rail is 44px wide with the panel's own mark at its head, the same 44px target the rest of the site's controls use. The same mark, aimed the other way, brings the panel back.
- **The Welcome page was substantially revised.** Spec-Driven Engineering is now introduced as software engineering plus AI, using an AI coding agent today and building one later, with the problem it solves, why checking stays with a person, and the stage table folded into one section instead of three. New sections cover what background you do not need, a what-you-need and what-you-do-not table, how the book's stage, chapter and lesson structure works and why it is ordered that way, and a roadmap-toned account of what is still being built. The call to start reading now sits at the very end of the page, after it has actually said what the book is.
- **Every table now reads as a raised card.** Rounded corners, a soft shadow, a header styled as a small-caps label instead of bold text on flat grey, and a hover highlight on each row. This is a sitewide style change, not a per-page one, so it also reaches the comparison tables already shipped in the chapters on the terminal and on spec-driven engineering, and in Stage 1's Core Programming and Backend chapters, not only the two new tables on the Welcome page.
- **Every table's header row is a dark, top-lit rail now, in the site's own metal finish.** The flat mid-grey fill it replaced carried grey labels that measured below the readable-contrast floor in both light and dark mode; the labels now clear it with room to spare. Nothing else about a table changed.
- **In dark mode the two side panels sit on the same ground.** The left sidebar and the On this page panel now share one tone, between two steps of the site's grey ramp rather than either step itself. The sidebar came down to it and the panel came up to meet it, so the tonal step that used to separate the two panels is gone. The sidebar's own labels got easier to read as a result, not harder. Light mode was untouched.
- **Light mode's page is no longer pure white.** The reading column now sits on a very light paper tone and the two document panels half a step deeper, so the ground reads as paper rather than glare and pure white is left to the surfaces meant to catch light. That moved light mode, which the note above deliberately left alone. The panels' small labels stepped one half-step darker to stay above the readable-contrast floor on their new ground. Dark mode was untouched here; its page stays pure black.

### 2026-09-14

#### New

- **Stage 0 became an official stage of the curriculum.** It sits before Stage 1 and covers computing history from binary through today's AI-coding-agent era, plus the Spec-Driven Engineering approach the rest of the book depends on. The curriculum was five stages (0 through 4) at that point, not four.
- **Chapters can now be labelled Theory or Practice.** A label is built into the site so you can tell at a glance whether a page teaches a concept or walks you through doing something. No chapter carries one yet; the labels go on as chapters are written.
- **Two new reference pages shipped: a [Code of Conduct](/code-of-conduct) and an [Accessibility Statement](/accessibility).** The accessibility statement names the real WCAG 2.2 AA baseline the site aims at, and the automated checks that keep it honest.
- **A [FAQ](/faq) page shipped**, independently checked against the rest of the site before publishing.

#### Changed

- **The homepage dashboard shows the stages, not a chapter count.** It was rebuilt to show each stage as it stood, and it dropped the old fixed chapter count. This book does not commit to a total chapter number in advance, because the curriculum keeps growing.
- **The homepage's two curriculum charts were rebuilt.** The bar chart shows each stage's real authoring status instead of a chapter count, and the donut shows the stages as equal parts of one whole instead of an hours breakdown. Both had been dropping Stage 0 and stating numbers this book does not commit to.
