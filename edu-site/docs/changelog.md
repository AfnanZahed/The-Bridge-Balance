# Changelog

A dated record of what actually shipped — a chapter going `text-ready`, a stage being promoted, a real platform feature landing. Internal work (refactors, lint fixes, a retried build) doesn't appear here; this page is for changes a reader would notice or care about.

### 2026-09-20

- **The four "why this book" cards on the homepage are cards now.** They had no styling at all, so the four headings and the four paragraphs under them ran together as one long column of text with nothing to separate one pillar from the next. Each is now a raised panel with a lit top edge, its number set large and faint in the corner, and its icon in a small raised chip of its own. The number is what identifies a card — the four top edges stepping from light grey to black in order are the same descent down the site's silver ramp the five stages use, so the row reads as one set. Moving the pointer over a panel brings a soft light with it.
- **[How a Machine Holds a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [How People Told a Machine What to Do](/ch02-programming-is-born) have been rewritten.** Both now open by welcoming you, saying what the chapter covers **and why it matters**, and then move into the teaching gradually instead of dropping you into it. Each one is now carried by one everyday thing you can picture the whole way through: a torch signalled across a dark street in the first, and a tireless helper with no judgment at all in the second. Hard words that the ideas did not need are gone — a transistor is "a tiny piece of specially treated crystal" now, not "a sliver of treated germanium." The history, the loom, the three kinds of switch, the rewiring of ENIAC and the arrival of FORTRAN are all still there.
- **Chapter and section titles now say what they are about.** "Yes, No, and Nothing In Between" is now "Why a Computer Only Says Yes or No." In the sidebar, "1. Foundations" is now "1. How a Machine Holds a Number," and "2. Programming Is Born" is now "2. How People Told a Machine What to Do." A title should tell you what is inside before you open it.
- **Stage 0 has a clearer name.** It was "Orientation Through Computing History." It is now **Introduction to Computing: From Switches to AI Agents** — the same stage, named so you can tell what it covers without reading it first.
- **The bold-every-first-sentence style is gone.** From 16 September, the opening sentence of almost every paragraph in the book was set in bold. The intention was to make a page easier to scan; the effect was that nothing stood out, because everything did. Bold now marks what actually matters, wherever in a paragraph it falls, and pages are made easier to scan with bullets, tables and shorter sections instead.
- **The glossary stops talking to itself.** Entries used to carry notes written for the book's own authors — "Beginner · Summary", "Advanced · Detailed (Surface 2 — Tool calling)". Those are gone; each entry now simply says where in the book you first meet the term.

### 2026-09-17

- [Introduction to Stage 0](/ch00-introduction) now opens Stage 0, and the Stage 0 entry in the sidebar lands on it. It is the stage's welcome page: what Stage 0 covers, grouped into the five stretches it is actually built from, what you'll be able to do at the end of it, what it costs (nothing to install, nothing to buy, no account), why checking an AI's work can't be handed to the machine, and an honest note that there is no quiz and no certificate here — the real credentials this book leads to are Stage 2's CS50P and CS50W. It closes by handing the reader into [How a Machine Learned to Hold a Number](/ch01-foundations), naming the traffic light and the telegraph key that chapter opens on. The book's original five chapters stay exactly where they were.
- [Introduction to Stage 0](/ch00-introduction) was then given a full Name-First pass the same day: it names itself the first of five stages, explains that the stage runs in the order computing developed — each idea after the problem that produced it — links [*Spec-Driven Engineering*](/glossary#spec-driven-engineering) where the book's four-step workflow is named, pointing at the stage's last chapter for the practice in full, and asks readers to take the chapters in order.
- [How a Machine Learned to Hold a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [From Wires to Words: How Programming Began](/ch02-programming-is-born) are now linked from the sidebar, at the end of Stage 0's list. The book's original five chapters — [intro-1](/intro-1-binary-to-programming) through [intro-5](/intro-5-spec-driven-engineering) — stay exactly where they were; nothing was removed or reordered, so both sequences are reachable while the rest of the new chronological ladder is written.
- Stage 1's six chapters (Foundations, Core Programming, Frontend, Backend, Databases, Git & GitHub) are back to `placeholder`. They had shipped `text-ready` on 2026-09-11; the site now shows the same "content to be added when the video series is recorded" note the rest of the stage's index carries. If you read any of that earlier text, treat it as withdrawn rather than current.

- [How a Machine Learned to Hold a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [From Wires to Words: How Programming Began](/ch02-programming-is-born) now open with an orientation instead of dropping you straight into a section. Each one says why its subject arrives now, lists the steps it moves through, and names what you will be able to do at the end of it. Both were also thinned out: the dates and full names that had piled up in the prose are gone wherever the story carries the point without them, and the run of paragraphs that each opened on a bolded sentence now reads as ordinary prose. The teaching is unchanged — the traffic light, the loom, the three switch bodies, the rewiring of ENIAC and FORTRAN are all still there. *(Both chapters were rewritten again on 20 September — see above. The traffic light became a torch, and the opening became a proper welcome.)*

### 2026-09-16

- A chapter's prose is set to a readable line length now. The reading column used to spread to whatever width the screen allowed, which at a desktop size put about 98 characters on a line — well past the 45–75 that reading comfortably holds, and the main reason a dense page read as a wall. It is capped so a line carries about 70 characters at any wide size, and the column sits centred with an even margin either side instead of pressed against the left of the leftover space. Nothing moves on a phone or a small laptop, where the line was already inside the band.
- Chapter titles and section headings are heavier, larger and underlined. Every heading level in use steps up 10%, reads at bold weight, and carries a hairline rule under it that runs the width of the text block — so a title that wraps to three lines gets one rule under the block, not one rule per line. The title's old short accent bar is gone, because two rules under one title is one rule too many.
- About one sentence in five of the book's prose is now set in bold, on one rule: the opening sentence of a paragraph, which is the sentence that says what the paragraph is about. Emphasis the text already carried is left exactly where it was, and a paragraph whose first sentence is a link or already emphasised takes the next sentence instead.
- A glossary term in the prose is marked quietly now instead of loudly. It used to carry four signals at once — the display serif, an accent colour, a full-strength underline, and a tinted background — which made a defined word read as a foreign object inside its own sentence. It stays in the reading ink and is marked by one thing: a fine dotted accent underline that turns to the accent colour when you hover it. Ordinary links are unchanged, so a term and a link no longer look alike at a glance.
- Italics in a chapter are emphasis again rather than a change of typeface, which is what let the glossary term above stop reading like a quotation. The display serif is kept for the one surface that wants it: a pull-quote inside a chapter, which now sets larger in that serif with a short accent rule above it, and no left border. List bullets take the same small accent diamond the headings use, and the space between paragraphs, headings, tables, quotes and code now steps by a single scale instead of eight unrelated numbers.
- The reading column's side margins are less than half the size they were, and the prose now takes back the space whenever a panel is folded away. It used to be a fixed width, which had two consequences: the empty margin was 119px per side at a desktop size, and folding the left sidebar made that margin *wider* — 216px — while the text stayed exactly where it was. The column now holds a constant margin and lets the text fill everything else, so it grows into whatever the panels free up and stops there. The line lands around 80 characters at a desktop size, against 71 before; a narrower margin would have reached 85, which is why it stops short of the 50% the margin was measured against.
- The **On this page** panel folds away now, the way the left sidebar does. A handle at its head names the action, and folded it leaves the same 44px rail carrying the same mark aimed the other way — so either edge of the page can be cleared for full-width reading, and the mark that folded it is the mark that brings it back. It moves on the same 200ms clock as the sidebar's rail, holds its own contrast in both light and dark mode, and remembers being folded as you move between chapters. The text widens to meet the space and narrows again when the panel returns.
- The first two chapters of a rebuilt Stage 0 have shipped: [How a Machine Learned to Hold a Number](/ch01-foundations) and [From Wires to Words: How Programming Began](/ch02-programming-is-born). They open a new, single chronological sequence — nothing taught before the thing it depends on — running from a two-state switch through the first working compiler in 1957. The book's original five-chapter Stage 0 stays live while the rest of the new sequence is written; these two chapters are not yet linked from the sidebar.

### 2026-09-15

- A [Welcome](/welcome) page now opens the book, ahead of Stage 0. It states the founding claim — what "balance" actually means, and why checking an AI agent's work can't move to the machine — inside the first screen, then what you need to start (nothing), how the five stages fit together, how a chapter is meant to be read, an honest list of what the site doesn't have yet (no community, no help chatbot, no certificate), and who actually writes and maintains the book.
- The Welcome page's key terms — *coding agent* and *Spec-Driven Engineering* — now link to the [Glossary](/glossary) at their first use, and its "why can't the checking move to the machine" section now names the gap between what the word "agent" implies (something that decides on its own) and what actually runs (software whose independence is a setting you control, not a trait it has).
- The reading page's type was rebalanced so headings lead it again. Body prose, code, tables, callouts, the sidebar and the "On this page" list all step down 20%, and the measure steps down with them so a line still holds the same number of characters. The reason: a section heading was rendering at 17.28px next to 17px body text, so the four levels read as one. They now sit on a single ladder — 13.6px body, then 17.28, 24.32 and 31.68 at h3, h2 and the chapter title — and the chapter title, which had fallen to within 3% of a section heading, carries the page the way a title should.
- The sidebar's collapse control is a labelled handle now, and the space it leaves behind is a designed rail. Collapsing used to leave a blank 30px strip with a grey arrow floating at its centre, while the control that did the collapsing was a full-width bar with square corners and a chevron pointing at nothing. Closing the panel is now a capsule at the panel's foot that names what it does, and the collapsed rail is 44px wide with the panel's own mark at its head — the same 44px target the rest of the site's controls use, and the same mark, aimed the other way, brings the panel back.
- The Welcome page was substantially revised. Spec-Driven Engineering is now introduced as software engineering plus AI — using an AI coding agent today, building one later — with the problem it solves, why checking stays with a person, and the five-stage table folded into one section instead of three. New sections cover what background you don't need, a what-you-need/what-you-don't table, how the book's stage/chapter/lesson structure works and why it's ordered that way, and a roadmap-toned account of what's still being built. The call to start reading now sits at the very end of the page, after it has actually said what the book is.
- Every table on the site now reads as a raised card instead of a bare default grid: rounded corners, a soft shadow, a header styled as a small-caps eyebrow instead of bold text on flat grey, and a hover highlight on each row. This is a sitewide style change, not a per-page one, so it also reaches the comparison tables already shipped in `intro-4`, `intro-5`, and Stage 1's core-programming and backend chapters — not only the two new tables on the Welcome page.
- The header row of every table is now a dark, top-lit rail in the site's own metal finish, with the column labels set in a light tone. The flat mid-grey fill it replaced carried grey labels that measured below the readable-contrast floor in both light and dark mode; the labels now clear it with room to spare. Nothing else about a table changed.
- In dark mode the left sidebar and the "On this page" panel now sit on the same ground — one tone between two steps of the site's silver ramp, rather than either step itself. The sidebar came down to it and the panel came up to meet it, so the tonal step that used to separate the two panels is gone. The sidebar's own labels got easier to read as a result, not harder. Light mode is untouched.
- Light mode's page is no longer pure white. The reading column now sits at silver-0.75, and the two document panels — the left sidebar and the "On this page" panel — at silver-1.5, so the ground reads as paper rather than glare and pure white is left to the surfaces meant to catch light. That moves light mode, which the note above deliberately left alone. The panels' small labels stepped one half-step darker to stay above the readable-contrast floor on their new ground. Dark mode is untouched here; its page stays pure black.

### 2026-09-14

- **Stage 0 — Orientation** is now an official stage of the curriculum, sitting before Stage 1 and covering computing history from binary through today's AI-coding-agent era, plus the Spec-Driven Engineering and Reading & Understanding Literacy philosophy the rest of the book depends on. The curriculum is five stages (0 through 4), not four.
- Every chapter and lesson now carries a **Theory** or **Practice** label, so a reader can tell at a glance whether a page teaches a concept or walks through doing something.
- The homepage curriculum dashboard now reflects five stages and drops the old fixed chapter count — this book never commits to a total chapter number in advance, since the curriculum keeps growing (see the site's own no-fixed-count rule).
- Two new reference pages: a [Code of Conduct](/code-of-conduct) and an [Accessibility Statement](/accessibility), the latter naming the real WCAG 2.2 AA baseline and the automated checks that enforce it.
- The homepage's two curriculum charts were rebuilt: the bar chart now shows each stage's real authoring status instead of a chapter count, and the donut shows the five stages as equal parts of one whole instead of an hours breakdown — both were dropping Stage 0 and stating numbers this book doesn't commit to.
- A [FAQ](/faq) page shipped, independently fact-checked against the repo before publishing.
