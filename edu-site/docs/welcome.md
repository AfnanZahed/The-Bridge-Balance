# Welcome

Welcome to The Bridge Balance — a free course that takes you from zero to real, working skill with a [*coding agent*](/glossary#agent). It's software that writes and runs code for you, largely on its own.

Don't trust everything it writes without checking it, and don't rewrite everything it gives you by hand either — both waste what a coding agent is actually good for. Use it for the part it does faster than you: writing the code. Keep the part only you can do: deciding what to build, and checking that what got built is right. That's what *balance* means in this book's name — not a split where some judgment goes to you and some to the agent, but exactly this discipline, every time. It has a name: [*Spec-Driven Engineering*](/glossary#spec-driven-engineering) — write down what you want and its limits before the agent writes anything, then check what comes back before you trust it.

You need no coding background to start. The book runs from how a computer works at its most basic level, through two genuine Harvard credentials, to real, practical work directing a coding agent yourself — this same discipline carries you through every stage of it.

Start now. Read [the first chapter](/intro-1-binary-to-programming). It needs nothing from you — no setup, no signup, no account, no prior programming experience.

## Why can't the checking move to the machine?

A travel agent decides where you fly. A real-estate agent decides which listings to show you. The word *coding agent* borrows that same sense of independent judgment. What actually runs is software that follows the boundaries it's given. The independence is a setting you control, not something the software has by itself.

A coding agent's answer reads as though it were true whether it is or not — the voice doesn't change when it's wrong. That confidence is the real risk, more than any single mistake.

Think of it against a courier's tracking page. A tracking page reports on your parcel from outside — a scanner, the carrier's own database, a system independent of the delivery itself. A coding agent's answer comes from the same process that wrote the code. There's no independent system checking its own work. Even a tracking page has a real limit worth naming: it confirms the parcel moved, never that what's inside is what you ordered. Opening the box stays yours to do.

That's why the checking can't move to the machine — there's nothing independent on the other end to hand it to. Spec-Driven Engineering bounds what the agent decides and leaves the checking with you, every time. Reading the code before you trust it is the habit this book builds from the first chapter onward.

## What you need before you start

Nothing. There's no signup and no account, because there's no sign-in anywhere on the site yet. No prior programming experience is needed, and the first chapter installs nothing.

## What does this book actually cover?

Five stages, in a fixed order, from the history of computing to autonomous AI agents. The [FAQ lists all five](/faq#what-are-the-five-stages) and what each one covers — this page just sits in front of the first.

Stage 2 carries the two credentials the curriculum is built around: Harvard's CS50P (Python) and CS50W (web). Both courses are free to work through, lectures and projects included. The verified certificate is the paid part, issued through edX, and edX sets the price — check the course page before you budget for it.

The five stages are fixed. The chapters inside them are not. Each chapter's topic and length is decided when it's written, so the book grows rather than being filled in from a list. No chapter count and no finishing date appears anywhere, on purpose.

## How to read this book

Straight through, one chapter at a time. Each one is a single continuous read with its own arc, and each defines its terms where they first appear — that's what lets a chapter stand on its own, even if you arrived from a search result with no earlier chapter behind you.

The pages are built to read well on a phone and to cost little data. Text comes first, images are light and few, and no video stands between you and the writing.

The book is self-paced. Come back months later, skip ahead, or re-read a chapter. Nothing expires, and nothing about your reading is tracked.

## What's not here yet

A front door that promises more than it holds is worse than none. Here's what the book doesn't have, as of now.

- **No community.** There's no Discord, no forum, and no chat. The public GitHub copy is a one-time snapshot, not a place to ask a question.
- **No help chatbot.** One is planned, and it would answer from the book's own chapters rather than from general knowledge. It isn't built.
- **No certificate from this book.** The Bridge Balance issues none, and it tracks no completion. The only portable credentials here are CS50's two, in Stage 2.
