## Rule checks

### No three-stat runs (Pacing §3.1)

The 50+ paragraphs are mostly `scene`, `teacher-aside`, `quote`, or `transition`. Where multiple stats appear in sequence (paragraphs 17, 18, 57, 58), each stat is **embedded inside a teacher-aside or scene-anchored paragraph** — i.e. the paragraph is classified as a stat only when the stat is the *primary* content. In every such run, the lead-in is a teacher-aside, so the rule is honored: no three consecutive paragraphs are pure-stat.

### Hook on every opener (Pacing §3.2)

Each H2 section opens with a scene, question, contradiction, or direct address:

- **Cold Open** — opens with "On a Tuesday in July 2025, Jason Lemkin…" (scene)
- **Extreme A** — opens with "Begin with a person you have probably never heard of…" (direct address)
- **Extreme B** — opens with "In April 2023, three engineers at Samsung Electronics…" (scene + date)
- **Shared Trap** — opens with "Both poles share a root…" (refrain / contradiction)
- **Bridge** — opens with "The cure is not more AI and it is not less AI…" (contradiction)
- **CTA** — opens with the who-this-is-for table (scanability; direct to reader)

### Closing transfer on every section (Pacing §3.3)

- Cold Open closes with "What happened in between is the subject of the rest of this introduction. It is told in two halves: the surrender pole, and the resistance pole."
- Extreme A closes with "But blind surrender has a sibling."
- Extreme B closes with "The resistance pole has not lost the argument. It has lost the time."
- Shared Trap closes with "If both poles are symptoms, the question is the cure."
- Bridge closes with "The cure is a discipline. Here is how the rest of this book teaches it."
- CTA closes with "The bridge is built on specifications. The crossing is yours."

### Sentence rhythm (Pacing §3.4)

Varied across paragraphs — short punchy sentences (e.g., "Both groups were right about something important. Both groups were also wrong about something important.") interspersed with longer reflective sentences (the Veracode and labor-market paragraphs). The glossary block in Extreme A uses a sentence-fragment definition style for terminology clarity.

### First 120 words open a question (Pacing §2.1)

The Cold Open opens a curiosity gap inside the first ~120 words: *"What did the person driving the agent miss that would have caught this?"* This question is paid off in the Shared Trap beat ("Both poles share a root. They agree that the human is not in charge.") and again in the Bridge beat ("It is the discipline of saying, in writing, what the system is supposed to do before the system exists.").

### No exclamation points, marketing clichés, quote stacking

- No `!` in body prose.
- Forbidden marketing words ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "the future is here", "don't get left behind") — none present.
- Quotes are spread across the page (Karpathy in Cold Open, SO mods in Resistance Pole, Majors/Gee/Goel in Resistance Pole, Beck in Bridge, DHH in Resistance Pole). No paragraph contains two verbatim quotes.
