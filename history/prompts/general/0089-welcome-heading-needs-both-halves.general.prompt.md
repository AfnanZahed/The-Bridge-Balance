## Prompt

"Fix this heading's text: What you need before you start, because the heading's feel is that the text below it will tell the needs, but the table also telling what don't needed, so make the text "What you need before you start" compatible with reality."

## Response snapshot

Confirmed the mismatch in the source first: `## What you need before you start` sat directly above a two-column table whose headers are `You need` and `You don't need`. So the heading promised one thing and the table delivered two, which is exactly the gap the owner described.

Checked for collateral before editing, per the project's rule that a rename must not leave anything pointing at a dead path. Searched the repo for both the heading text and its generated anchor. The only references were the generated `static/search-index.json` (which the build rewrites from the docs, so it self-heals) and two historical PHRs — `0086`, which recorded creating this section, and `0077`, which recorded the earlier decision to keep this header flat while two others became questions. Both quote the old wording as a record of what was decided at the time, so both were left untouched; rewriting a PHR to match a later change would falsify the history it exists to keep.

Read `curriculum-state/canon/voice.md` before choosing the wording, since the project treats headings as voice-bearing rather than as labels. The rules that bear on a heading like this: second person and active voice; cut every word that survives its own deletion; no triads; and the contrast-pair reflex ("not just X, but Y") is a banned tell after its first use in a chapter. That ruled out a symmetric flourish and left the smallest honest edit — keep the original phrase whole and name the half that was missing:

`## What you need before you start, and what you don't`

This mirrors the table's own two headers (`You need` / `You don't need`), so the heading now describes the table beneath it exactly. Chose a comma over an em-dash aside, and plain wording over a rewrite, since the owner asked for the existing text to be made compatible with reality rather than replaced.

No other line of the page changed.

**Deliberately not changed: the changelog.** The entry already live under 2026-09-15 describes this section as "a what-you-need/what-you-don't table". In other words the changelog already described the section correctly and the *heading* was the thing out of step with it, so there is nothing for the changelog to correct — and adding a bullet for a four-word heading edit would be exactly the kind of internal churn that page says it excludes. Flagged here rather than done silently, in case the owner wants it logged anyway.

- 🧪 Tests: See `tests:` above — full build green with all content gates passing (0 errors, 1 pre-existing warning), the new anchor confirmed in the built HTML, and the live dev-server render confirmed in the browser rather than inferred.

Both are accurate and they cover different scopes (background vs. practical prerequisites), but two headings in a row leaning on "don't" is worth a look if the owner wants the page's header rhythm tighter.

It said the table covers both what you need and what you don't, which reframed the task from "a heading needs improving" to "the heading had drifted out of step with the page's own description of it" — and it also settled the question of whether the changelog needed a new entry. Two sources of truth disagreeing is a better signal than either one alone.
