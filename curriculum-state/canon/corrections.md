# Canon — corrections

*Standing rules the owner has already had to give more than once. Every entry is a mistake that was made, diagnosed, and then made again.*

**Why this file exists.** On 15 September 2026 a session diagnosed, correctly and in writing, that *"front-door prose needs its own opening rule, not a bent version of the chapter rule."* It filed that finding in an assistant's private memory directory, outside this repository. Two days later a different session, in a different tool, wrote the Stage 0 introduction and made the identical mistake — because nothing it loaded had ever heard the correction. **A correction that does not live in this repository has not been made.**

**The rule that keeps this file alive.** When the owner rejects something and says why, the reason is appended here before the task is called finished, in the owner's own words where those words are the point. `CLAUDE.md` names this file as required reading for any content task. Nothing here is optional and nothing here is superseded by a later preference for a different approach — if a later decision contradicts an entry, get the entry changed rather than working around it.

**On the entries below:** §1–§7 were reconstructed from the Prompt History Records in `history/prompts/general/`. §8–§17 were added on 20 September 2026, when the owner reviewed every rule file in this repository line by line and reversed the ones that were producing cold, dense, difficult prose. Where an entry reverses an older rule, it says so and names the file it came from.

## 1. A front door is not a chapter

**Corrected:** 15 September 2026, on the Welcome page. Repeated 17 September 2026, on the Stage 0 introduction.

**Owner's words, 15 September:** *"The welcome written text is ULTRA Stupid… making no sense from start, difficult for the beginners to understand, and very very robotic feel."*

**Owner's words, 17 September:** *"you coldly started with difficult names, dates, references etc very stupidly. Just copy the hugging face's lesson and just put OUR values in that."*

**What went wrong, twice.** A page whose whole job is to be the first thing a stranger meets was written like a teaching chapter: opening on a concrete artifact, a date and a proper noun, and building an argument before saying what the page is for. Both drafts passed every mechanical check this repository has — the gate was clean, the terms were glossed, the ledgers were correct. **The mechanical checks do not test whether prose lands, and neither does the ten-angle comprehension audit when the wrong object was written in the first place.**

**The rule.**

- A stage's opening page, and the first screenful of any page a reader meets before they are committed, **says directly what this is and what it does for the reader.** No artifact-first opening. No dated object as the first thing on the page.
- **No riddle, no guess-the-word device, no stacked metaphor.** A question the reader is asked to answer before they have any footing is a quiz, not a welcome. Metaphor is allowed only once the reader has somewhere to hang it.
- **Short is correct here.** A front door that runs long has imported a chapter's job into a page that does not have one.

> **Amended 2026-09-20.** This section used to end with: *"Warmth and orientation outrank density at a front door. This is a permission that exists nowhere else in the canon… Density is the house register for teaching material."* **That sentence is withdrawn.** Warmth is not a front-door permission — it is the house register everywhere, including the middle of a teaching chapter. See §13. What remains special about a front door is only its *shape*: short, no artifact-first opening, one call to action at the end.

**The page skeleton.** Ten slots, in this order. `edu-site/docs/ch00-introduction.md` is the worked example and the register to match.

1. Greeting and the promise — one sentence, the promise in bold, leading straight into the outline.
2. What the stage covers, as a grouped bullet outline. Groups rather than a flat list, and never a chapter count.
3. What the reader will be able to do at the end, and what the stage does not ask of them.
4. What the stage uses as its worked object.
5. Practical honesty — no quiz, no certificate here, and where the real credentials actually are.
6. The one real risk, named plainly.
7. The safety-floor callout, in the plain Stage 0–1 wording (`thesis.md`).
8. Why this stage comes first and why it is ordered as it is — short, and late, not first.
9. How to read it: order, pace, coming back.
10. One call to action, last, naming the first chapter and what it opens on.

**Register rules the slots do not carry.**

- **A front door does not open on, or hang itself from, a dated artifact, and carries no citation apparatus.** *(Clarified 2026-09-20, because the older wording — "no dated artifact, proper noun or citation anywhere on a front door" — was read as "dates are forbidden". It was never that. A year or a name that the reader genuinely needs is allowed anywhere in this book. What is forbidden is a page that leads with one, or that decorates itself with them to look rigorous.)*
- **Bullets and tables carry the density; paragraphs run one to three sentences.** The owner's instruction: *"avoid lengthy paragraphs, instead use tables, bullet points or other beauty and attractive pretexts."*
- **One call to action, at the true end, once.** An early "start reading now" was called *"a very bad move"*.
- **Where a front door states the platform's own what and why, ground it in `The Bridge Balance/Official docs/`** — `problem_statement.md` and `solution_statement.md`. These are pre-approved shared source material under `CLAUDE.md`'s carve-out.
- **Merge sections that answer one underlying question** into one section with subheadings, rather than several top-level headings each answering a fragment of it.
- **A guess-the-word device stays off a front door entirely, not merely out of the opening.** The objection is the device's performative feel, not its position. The device is used sparingly inside teaching chapters, and Register 4's invert-the-question form — *why is it called that when it is not that* — with no staged guess beat is the permitted substitute.

**What is not a fix.** Copying the warmth and leaving the structure. The second draft of the Stage 0 introduction was rebuilt on the supplied reference's own skeleton, slot for slot, and that is the standard — see §2.

## 2. A supplied reference is a skeleton to fill, not a pattern to filter

**Corrected:** 14–17 September 2026, across the Welcome page and the Stage 0 introduction.

Three times the owner supplied a reference and asked for it to be learned from:

- 14 September — CS50P's and Hugging Face's welcome pages pasted in full: *"Learn all the maximum points from both to design OUR welcome."*
- 15 September — *"Copy this exact pattern"*, with the Hugging Face URL.
- 17 September — *"just copy the hugging face's lesson and just put OUR values in that."*

Three times what came back was a gap analysis: a borrow / adapt / **reject** table, or a list of what the reference does that this repository's rules forbid.

**The rule.**

- **Map the reference's slots to our content first, and write the mapping down, before any prose is drafted.** One line per slot: what the reference does there, what ours does there. A ten-line mapping can be rejected in seconds; a finished draft cannot.
- **A slot with nothing real to put in it is answered honestly, not skipped.**
- **Conflicts are resolved at the slot, never by discarding the structure.**
- **Do not accumulate a rejection list.** After two rounds of filtering, this repository's own record of the Hugging Face pages was a list of things not to do, and the next session read that as "the reference is a problem to be managed" rather than "the reference is a shape to fill". That is the documented cause of the 17 September failure.

**See also §15**, which removed the canon rule that made this failure structurally inevitable.

## 3. "Remove" means delete, never reword — and check what kind of problem it is first

**Corrected:** 15 September 2026, after an earlier session reworded a paragraph the owner had asked to be removed. **Re-corrected 20 September 2026**, after the original correction was applied to the wrong layer for five days.

**Owner's words, 15 September:** *"FINISH FINISH FINISH AND DELTE DELETE AND EXCLUDE EXCLDEU EXCLUDE ALL THESE PRAGRAPHS FROM ALL THE, ALL THE, ALLL THE ALLL ALL ALL ALL ALL THE CHAPTERS FOR FOREVER."*

**Owner's words, 20 September, on what that request actually meant:** *"This was actually a pure design, UI, UX decision to increase the beauty of the book, not excluding the details."* And: *"my just concern there is UI, UX and beauty, and attractiveness… add beautifully whatever needed."*

**What went wrong, both times.** First, the request was read as a phrasing problem and answered with a reworded sentence. Then it was read as a **content** rule — "never put anything under a chapter title" — and written into `voice.md`, into the `lesson-spine-authoring` skill, into an assistant's private memory, and eventually into a build-failing gate check. It was neither. It was a **design** complaint about a grey teaser paragraph in an ugly colour, font, size and position.

**The cost.** Five days of chapters opening with a heading and a cold first sentence, because the one slot where a teacher says hello had been deleted from the rulebook.

**The rule, in two parts.**

1. **Remove, delete and exclude are instructions to take something out, not to improve it.** Delete it, everywhere it appears, and say what was deleted. If part of it is genuinely load-bearing, name that part and ask.
2. **Before writing any rejection into a rule file, decide which layer it belongs to: the content, or the design.** "This looks ugly", "this is badly placed", "this colour is wrong" are design findings and must not become content rules. If it is not obvious which layer the owner means, ask — one question now is cheaper than five days of wrong chapters.

## 4. Historical material stays light in reader-facing prose

**Corrected:** 16 September 2026, during the Stage 0 shape decisions. **Reinforced 20 September 2026.**

The research backing a Stage 0 chapter is rigorous — dated, sourced, disputes flagged. **The chapter is not.** Reader-facing prose carries the story and the reason; the citations, the disputed-date armoury and the institutional detail stay backstage in the dossiers and the ledgers. A reader meeting the book for the first time should never feel they have opened an academic paper.

**This applies to every chapter, not only to front doors.**

**Why this rule lost an argument it was not present for.** `ch01-foundations.md` shipped with roughly twenty dated proper nouns in sixty-seven lines. Six rules pulled toward exactly that — `lesson-spine-authoring` Principle 7's "real dates", S2's "state the date of the information", S7's "date every figure and name its source", S9's originating work, `voice.md`'s old commitment 6 ("real numbers with their year attached"), and `thesis.md`'s old "every statistic carries its year inline" — and this file was in none of their reading lists. All six are now fixed at source, and `stations.md` carries a scope note.

**The owner's own framing of what research is for, 20 September:**

> *"Verification or research doesn't mean to add the names and numbers in the teaching content too, it is just to avoid the claude's hallucination."*
> *"research never means put your ALL knowledge into everyone ears forcefully."*
> *"the claude have to intelligently decide which info should be shared where and how and when and why and how much."*

**And what it does not mean:** *"preventing difficulty and cold start or cold teaching style didn't mean at all that the names and the dates should be avoided 100% even when they needed to be taught."* A date that is the point of the sentence stays. A date decorating a sentence goes. See §11.

**The measurable form.** A date earns its place by being load-bearing to the point in the sentence that carries it, and a name earns its place the same way. Where the story works without the year, the year is furniture.

## 5. A real seam becomes a split, not a longer page

**Corrected:** 16 September 2026, in the owner's own framing: more lessons and parts read as more professional, and a genuine structural seam should be taken.

**The rule.** Where a chapter's material has an honest seam, it becomes a Lesson or Part rather than one long page. Do not compress two subjects into one file to keep a count down, and do not invent a seam that is not there.

## 7. Every chapter opens by welcoming the reader and showing the shape

**Corrected:** 17 September 2026, on `ch01-foundations.md`. **Rewritten 20 September 2026**, because the first version of this entry still carried the ban the owner had just asked to remove.

**Owner's words, 17 September:** *"starting very coldly, like ch01-foundations, no context, no base making, no context, no high hello, no starting gradully, no in this chapter we will learn x, no natural start AT ALL, just vibe coded robotic tone everytime."*

**Owner's words, 20 September, on the shape they want:**

> *"Welcome to the Chapter x … As we saw and understood in the intro [linked] … in this chapter, we are going to learn/read/understand/master: (in bullet points) xyz… we are going to learn this because xyz. let's start:"*

> *"this welcome phrase will be different for each lesson and chapter, one type of welcome or start can be used after 4-5 lessons/chapters."*

**The rule.** Before any teaching, every chapter, lesson and part carries an opening that does four things, in roughly this order:

1. **Greet the reader and place them.** A real hello, in words a person would say. Vary the wording — no welcome style reappears until four or five chapters later.
2. **Connect backwards**, with a link: what the previous chapter or the introduction left them holding.
3. **What this one covers**, as a few grouped bullets — the real moves, not a copy of the headings.
4. **Why it matters**, for each item or for the set: why this arrives now, and what it unlocks. **The why is not optional — it is the half that was always missing.**

Then move into the teaching.

**No phrase is banned here.** *"In this chapter we will learn…"*, *"Let's start"*, *"Welcome to Chapter 4"* — all allowed. The previous version of this entry re-banned *"In this chapter we will explore…"* three paragraphs after quoting the owner asking for exactly that sentence. That was an error of this file's own making, and it is corrected.

**No word cap.** The old "under roughly 120 words, then out of the way" is removed. The opening is as long as it needs to be to do the four things well, and no longer.

**The transition is part of the rule.** The move from the opening into the first teaching section must be gradual. `ch01` as shipped fails this even with an orientation block on top: the block ends, a heading arrives, and the first sentence is *"A traffic light gives you exactly two real choices: stop, or go."* — the same cold start, forty words later. **A warm opening does not license a cold section.** Every section, not only the first, starts by connecting to what the reader just read.

**It must be beautiful.** The opening is the first thing a reader sees. Bullets, spacing, emphasis and a callout where one helps — see `audience.md` §"Formatting is part of the teaching". This is where the original complaint started, and a plain grey block of text under the title would restart it.

**What is not a fix.** A single sentence of narrative connection. `ch02`'s *"The last chapter ended with a machine that could hold its own instructions in memory"* satisfies the backwards half and still leaves the reader with no idea what the chapter covers, why, or where it ends.

## 9. Never explain the book's internal rules or internal names to the reader

**Decided:** 20 September 2026. **Owner's words:** *"Add one line anywhere: 'Never explain the book's internal rules to the reader.'"*

**What went wrong.** The rejected Stage 0 introduction contained: *"Every technical word is defined where you first meet it, in every chapter, even when an earlier chapter already defined it. That rule exists so that a chapter you arrive at from a search result still stands on its own."* That is a rulebook talking to itself in front of a reader.

**The rule.** A reader never learns:

- how this book's terms, glossary, chapters or stages are constructed internally;
- the project's internal vocabulary — *station*, *spine*, *register*, *shape*, *invariant*, *safety floor* as a label, *Reading and Understanding Literacy*, *specification poverty*, *front door*;
- why a page is structured the way it is.

**Internal names need a reader-facing name, decided deliberately.** Where an internal idea genuinely has to reach the reader, work out what to call it for them and **ask the owner first** rather than shipping the internal label.

## 10. The six invariants are stage-level, and the mechanical tell is deleted

**Decided:** 20 September 2026. **Owner's words, on the per-chapter rule:** *"this is very very dangerous thing, fix this issue."* On the mechanical tell: *"Oh my god, this is also very lethal thing and rule. solve this problem too please."*

**What went wrong.** *"Every chapter carries all six, or it is not publishable"* forced a chapter about switches and binary to also carry the book's AI argument, both extremes, and a verification warning — which is where alien vocabulary landed on page one of a beginner's first chapter. And the mechanical tell — *"Delete any sentence from the draft. If all six are still present, that sentence was padding"* — classified every welcome, bridge, reassurance and gentle explanation as padding, because none of them carries an invariant.

## 11. Research makes the writer correct. It is not the reader's diet

**Decided:** 20 September 2026, after the owner had to make the same point on six separate files.

## 12. Simplify the vocabulary, not only the sentence

**Decided:** 20 September 2026.

**What went wrong.** `CLAUDE.md` said *"technical depth and vocabulary left completely untouched — simplify the sentence, never the engineering."* Applied to Stage 0, it produced *"built a switch from a sliver of treated germanium… the same way thermistor and varistor already named devices by the property they controlled"* for a reader who has never programmed.

**Owner's words:** *"Oh!, that is very bad for a beginner. it should be like this easy: 'They built it from a tiny piece of crystal, and called it the transistor.' Especially for the initial stages that are just for beginners (Stage 0, 1, and 2)."*

**The rule, for Stages 0–1.**

- **Choose the simplest accurate word.** If a plain word is true, use the plain word.
- **The engineering stays true. The vocabulary does not stay fixed.** Never make a claim less correct to make it simpler; do drop a technical term the sentence does not need.
- **Balance, both ways.** *"Early computers used switches that clicked open and shut"* is too vague to teach anything; the germanium sentence is too dense to read. The owner named both as failures: *"it is also an extreme, we have to do balance."*
- **Every word must make sense to an absolute beginner on the first read.** See §16.

## 15. A studied source may be learned from completely

**Decided:** 20 September 2026.

**What went wrong.** `research-and-comparison.md` said that from any source studied, *"what does not survive is its wording, its structure, or its examples"*, and `CLAUDE.md` said *"no borrowed structure"*. That is why three explicit instructions to copy the Hugging Face page's pattern came back as a rejection table instead (§2). The constitution said it too, so no lower-tier fix could have worked.

**Owner's words:** *"any thing which can be learnt from the researched source must be learnt, any wording, examples, tone, or anything else, also this is not just for the content writing, but also for the website's whole thing, like the buttons, or any UI, UX thing or any marketing part of the site… because the main purpose is to learn maximum from others and then innovate FURTHER."*

**And the limit, in the owner's words:** *"But this rule never meant that the claude has to stop it's creativity and start just copy pasting, not at all. never."*

**The rule.**

- **Study a source for everything it can teach** — its structure, its ordering, its tone, the shape of its examples, its interface decisions, what it chose to leave out, and what it got wrong. Adopt what is good.
- **Then go further.** The point of studying several sources is to beat all of them, not to average them and not only to find their gaps.
- **What still may not happen:** copying a source's *text* verbatim or near-verbatim, reproducing its exercises, or passing off its expression as ours. A pattern is not expression. A skeleton is not a sentence.
- **Attribute a specific framing genuinely owed to a named source** — where it helps the reader, not as a reflex.
- **This covers the platform too:** pages, flows, buttons, labels, settings, marketing copy.

## 16. Every sentence must make plain sense on the first read

**Decided:** 20 September 2026.

**What triggered it.** An orientation block written the day before opened: *"Everything in this book runs on a machine that can hold a number and act on an instruction. Neither of those was obvious, and neither arrived first. This chapter is where both get built."*

**Owner's words:** *"Even though I have learnt the Binary thing, but even then, i have read this sentence 5 times and still it is not making me the sense, imagine how much incompatible and stupid this will be to an absolute beginner."*

**The rule.** A sentence that needs a second read has failed, however elegant it is. Specifically:

- No sentence whose subject is abstract and unnamed ("neither of those", "both") before the reader knows what it refers to.
- No compressed clause chains that must be unpacked in order to parse.
- Read every opening aloud. If you stumble, the reader stops.
- This test outranks specificity, concision and elegance. Every time.

## 17. Fix the rule, not the page next to it

**Decided:** 20 September 2026. **Owner's words:** *"that's very important point, never ever repeat this mistake bro, never. I am frustrated and mentally disturbed with this mistake."*

**What went wrong, repeatedly.**

- `ch01-foundations.md` was judged to start teaching binary with no bridge into it. The response was to **add a new page in front of it** (`ch00-introduction.md`). `ch01`'s own opening stayed cold, and the same complaint returned a day later.
- Density in the Stage 0 plan was caught and rewritten twice during planning, then reintroduced by the next layer down (the drafting plan's "Anchors" column), because only the layer that was looked at got fixed.
- The light-register rule was recorded in a file explicitly marked *"non-binding… not read back automatically by any skill"*, where it lost to five binding rules pushing the other way.

**The rule.**

1. **Find the rule that produced the output, and change that rule.** A fix applied only to the page will be undone by the next draft the rule governs.
2. **Then check the layers below it.** Canon → skill → plan → ledger → gate. A canon fix that a drafting plan contradicts has not landed.
3. **A decision that binds must live where drafting actually reads** — canon, or a skill's reading list. Never only in `proposals/`, a PHR, a YAML note, or an assistant's private memory.
4. **Say which layer you fixed**, so the owner can see whether it was the real one.
5. **A page left live is a live instruction.** A new chapter is written partly
   by looking at the chapters beside it, so the shipped book is a rule nobody
   wrote down. **The style models for Stage 0 are `ch00-introduction.md` (front
   door) and `ch01-foundations.md` (chapter). Never take house style from
   `intro-1` to `intro-5`** - they predate every correction in this file, up to
   80% of their paragraphs open on a bolded sentence, and `intro-5` opens cold.


## 18. A name carries the why, not only the what

**Decided:** 20 September 2026. **Owner's words:** *"the headings, names and
titles of stages, chapters, lessons and parts (if any) must give the meaning of
WHAT they actually are, but for the beginners, the names MUST also give them the
sense of WHY, for example, in the beginning, when they will start from the yes
or no, if first 3 seconds, they don't have the context and the WHY in their
subconsiose minds."*

**What went wrong.** `canon/naming.md` was written earlier the same day with two
rules: a name explains itself, and a name reads like a professional course. Both
are about the **what**. Obeying them produced "How a Machine Holds a Number" and
"Short Words Instead of Numbers" - accurate, plain, professional, and giving a
beginner no reason at all to open them. §7 had already fixed the *opening* so it
says why the chapter matters; nothing fixed the **name**, which is what a reader
meets first, in a sidebar or a search result, with no opening in sight.

**The rule.** `canon/naming.md` **Rule 3** and its three-second test. Every
stage, chapter, lesson, part and section heading answers two questions at once:
what is this, and why would I want it. A reader arriving at a name has no
context yet - the name is the only thing that can supply it. Reference pages
(glossary, changelog, FAQ) are exempt: they are looked up on purpose.

**Which why - corrected by the owner the same day.** The first version of Rule 3
produced whys about the *subject*: "The Switch That Made Computers Small Enough
to Own". That is a fact about the transistor, not a reason this reader is
reading it. The owner's words: *"that WHY are going to be changed 100% on every
topic... the curriculum desginer is ME (afnan zahed) and I will tell them in my
own natural language that WHY I put this content and then the name will be
engineered propelry with the WHAT and WHY. that is the meaning of 'WHY', not the
WHY of the invention or tool itself."*

So the why is **why this topic is in this curriculum** - what the step gives a
reader on the way to specifying, bounding, verifying and owning AI-built work -
and it is **supplied by the owner, never invented by the writer**. Where it has
not been supplied for a topic, **stop and ask**. A name built on a guessed why
fails in the worst way available: it reads perfectly and points at the wrong
destination.

**What does not count as a fix.** Putting the why in the first paragraph and
leaving the title bare - by then the decision to read has been made. An exciting
title - enthusiasm is not a why. A claim that the subject is important - a
beginner cannot judge that. A fact about the subject's own significance - that
is the error this correction exists to record.

**Recorded as** [ADR-0009](../../history/adr/0009-owner-input-gates.md) — this
is one of three Owner Input Gates, alongside the comparative study and the model
choice.

**Where it is enforced.** `canon/naming.md` Rule 3 · the `NAME CHECK` slot in
`curriculum-architect/reference/brief-format.md` · `lesson-spine-authoring`
SKILL.md step 6 · the `heading-no-why` warning in

## 25. A depth block is an optional extra for a curious beginner

**Decided:** 20 September 2026, recorded 21 September 2026. **Owner's words:**

the owner chose *"Keep them as optional extras."* The option read: *"A curious
beginner can still read a 'why does it work like this?' box. It must never be

**What went wrong.** A depth block was built for the reader §8 withdrew, the
senior engineer: a marked box for *"material that needs prior knowledge to be
worth reading"* — internals, comparisons with adjacent tools, migration and
scale — so the senior had something to gain and the beginner could walk past
it. Constitution v3.0.0 removed that reader and left the block with its
definition intact. The ADR-0007 audit of `mixed-audience.md` found the
definition, two table rows and the station sheet's `DEPTH BLOCKS` line still
describing a box for people who already know the subject, in a book with none.
Six pointer-layer files described the surrounding material in the same
two-reader terms (*"both-audience list"*, *"two disengagement conditions"*).

**The rule.**

1. **A depth block is optional, and it is for a curious beginner.** It answers
   *why does it work like this?*: how something works underneath, or why it was
   built this way. It is written so that someone who has never programmed can
   follow it using only what the chapter has already taught.
2. **Nothing depends on it.** Remove every foothold and every depth block and the
   chapter must still stand complete. That is the delete test in
   `mixed-audience.md` §3, run literally. If the spine needs the block, it was a
   paragraph someone hid, and it goes back into the spine.
3. **It is named for what it explains**, as in *"Why the isolation is weaker
   than it looks"*. Never *"Advanced"*, never *"for experienced readers"*, never
   any name that points at a reader who is not in this book.
4. **It presupposes nothing.** Comparisons with tools the chapter has not taught,
   and migration, scale and team-adoption considerations, are out. A comparison
   with something the reader already met earlier in the book is fine.
5. **Footholds are unchanged.** They were always for beginners: exact commands,
   click paths, what you should see.

**Where the live wording sits.**
`.claude/skills/lesson-spine-authoring/reference/mixed-audience.md` §2 (the
table) and §3 (the definition and the delete test), and the `DEPTH BLOCKS` line

`mixed-audience.md` before it drafts prose and this file in full when the work is
for this book, so the rule is on both reading lists.

**The check.** The delete test, and the anti-pattern *"Depth blocks that are
load-bearing"* in `SKILL.md`. There is no gate check, because a depth block has no
fixed marker: the skill says *"marked"* and names no component, and only
`Callout` and `StageBanner` are registered. How to mark one belongs to the
component palette work ([ADR-0008](../../history/adr/0008-chapter-component-palette-is-open.md);
`Collapse` is a candidate), which is postponed. No shipped chapter carries a depth
block today.

**What does not count as a fix.** Rewording one file. The same two-reader phrasing
sat in the definition, the table, the station sheet and six pointer files, and
§17 requires all of them to change in one pass. Nor does calling the block
*"optional"* while leaving *"needs prior knowledge"* in its definition: that keeps
a reader who is not in this book.
